Product Thinking · Lesson 1 of IDG pathway

Problems before solutions

The win: by the end, you can take a "let's build an AI chatbot" request and push it back to the real problem underneath.

1. The idea in one line

Singapore's Institute of Digital Government frames product thinking as asking better questions before reaching for a solution — define, scope and frame the problem first (IDG, Product Thinking). Its first module puts it bluntly: Great digital products start with problems, not solutions. (Module 1)

Why it matters for AI work: a team can execute brilliantly and still fail, because it solved the wrong problem.

2. Three principles

PrincipleWhat it means in practice
Problems before solutionsDiagnose first. "Use GenAI" is not a problem.
Outcomes over outputsSuccess = the change achieved (e.g. fewer repeat calls), not features shipped (e.g. "chatbot launched").
Policy–Ops–Tech integrationPolicy, operations and technology people work on the problem together, with clear accountability.

Source: IDG Module 1. Successful products often began by solving one specific problem for one defined user group, then expanded.

3. The tool: Five Whys

Ask "why?" repeatedly to move from symptom to cause (Module 2). An illustrative example (mine, not IDG's):

Request: "Build an AI chatbot for our hotline."
  1. Why? — Call volume is overwhelming officers.
  2. Why? — Most calls ask about application status.
  3. Why? — Applicants can't see their status anywhere.
  4. Why? — The status lives in a back-office system with no citizen view.
  5. Why? — Nobody owned the post-submission experience.
Maybe the fix is a status notification, not a chatbot.

IDG's caution: problems rarely have a single root cause. Pick a cause that is within your control and closely connected to the outcome you want to change.

4. Check yourself

Answer from memory — no scrolling up. Effortful recall is what makes it stick.

5. Try it on something real

Pick a problem from your own work or your agentic-AI project. Write five whys. Then circle the one cause you can actually act on.
